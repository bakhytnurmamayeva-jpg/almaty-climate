Is Almaty getting warmer?

A statistical look at 36 years of daily temperatures: correlation, regression and a hypothesis test.

Loading weather data from Open-Meteo…

Research question

Is there a statistically significant relationship between the year and the average annual temperature in Almaty between 1990 and 2025?

Hypotheses

H₀: ρ = 0. There is no linear relationship between year and temperature.
H₁: ρ ≠ 0. There is a linear relationship between year and temperature.
Significance level: α = 0.05.

Method

Daily mean temperatures for Almaty (43.24° N, 76.89° E) are averaged into one value per year. We then calculate descriptive statistics, the Pearson correlation coefficient r, a simple linear regression Temperature = a + b · Year, and test H₀ with a two-sided t-test on r. Winter and summer are analysed separately for comparison.

Data

Source: Open-Meteo Historical Weather API (variable temperature_2m_mean, reanalysis data). Each row is one year. Winter is December, January and February of that calendar year; summer is June, July and August.

Conclusion

What r, b and the p-value mean

r measures how closely the points follow a straight line, from −1 to +1. The closer to +1, the more consistently temperature rises with the years.
b is the slope of the regression line: the average change in temperature per year.
p-value is the probability of seeing a relationship at least this strong if there were really no relationship. If p < 0.05 we reject H₀.

Limitations

Correlation does not prove causation: this study shows that temperature changed over time in this data, not why. Only one location and 36 years are used, and reanalysis data is a model-based estimate rather than a single weather station. Year-to-year weather is also noisy, so a short period can hide or exaggerate a long-term trend.
